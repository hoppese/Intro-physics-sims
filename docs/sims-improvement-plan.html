The catalog page was completely broken

Found while adding the new electrostatics sims to the browse page. Two separate bugs, both pre-existing (not introduced this session), now fixed and pushed.

index.html never rendered at all. Its bootstrap loader was a stale build that unconditionally fetches React from unpkg.com — every one of the 43 sim pages was already updated to vendor React locally and never touch the network, but the catalog page itself was left on the old loader. With no live connection to that CDN (exactly the kind of spotty-wifi situation that started this conversation), the homepage just showed a blank page with a red "[bundle] error" toast — nothing else. Replaced the loader with the modern one, vendored local React/ReactDOM copies into its manifest, and populated the resource map so it never touches the network. Verified booting with zero network requests.
15 of 43 real, working sims were never added to the browse list — including 3 of the electrostatics sims just audited below (Equipotential Explorer, Moving Charge Field, E-Fields in Conductors), plus most of Mechanics and both Gravitation sims. Four whole unit categories (Mechanics & Motion, Gravitation & Orbits, Thermal Physics, EM Radiation) didn't even exist as filter buttons, despite being in the README's own sim list the whole time. Added the categories and all 15 cards. Also removed two dead placeholder cards left over from early planning that had never had a working link, and fixed a malformed URL on Standing Waves.

Net effect: the catalog now works offline, and every sim you've built is actually reachable from it. This was silently broken before tonight regardless of wifi — worth a five-minute look to confirm it matches what you expect.

03

New sims — drafted and delivered

All three sims suggested earlier are now built as standalone drafts, sent to you as files (not committed to the repo/catalog yet). Each has a working Info panel, Worked Example, and the same visual language as the production sims.

Parallel-Plate Capacitor ✓ built

C = κε₀A/d, Q = CV, U = ½CV². The centerpiece is a Battery-connected / Disconnected toggle: with the battery connected, sliding in a dielectric grows Q at fixed V; disconnected, the same dielectric drops V at fixed Q — verified both directions numerically (5× the capacitance → 5× the charge at fixed V, or 1/5 the voltage at fixed Q, energy dropping to 1/5 as the dielectric is pulled in by the field). One known rough edge: the dielectric slab's on-screen width scales with κ, which could read as "bigger κ = physically bigger slab" — worth a quick fix before this goes live.

Doppler Effect ✓ built

f' = f·(v±v_o)/(v∓v_s), with a Source-moves / Observer-moves toggle. The wavefront-ring animation correctly bunches ahead of a moving source and stays symmetric (just swept through faster) for a moving observer — the key conceptual distinction between the two cases. Includes a real Web Audio tone that pitch-shifts live as the source crosses the listener. Found and fixed one bug while testing: the fixed "ear" icon was drawing on top of the walking-observer icon in observer mode.

Beats ✓ built

Plots the two pure tones, their sum (the actual pressure wave), and the envelope, plus a real 3-second loudness strip so the beat rate is countable by eye. Two-tone Web Audio playback lets you actually hear the "wa-wa-wa" pulsing. Found and fixed one bug while testing: the Info/Worked-example buttons were completely unwired (a genuine oversight, not a framework issue) — now fixed.

Checked and confirmed not a gap: conductor shielding / Faraday cages are already covered inside E-Fields in Conductors & Insulators.

04

Interactivity & pedagogy

The strongest pattern in this codebase is that several sims already do something great that the rest don't. The cheapest, lowest-risk improvement across the board is generalizing those, not inventing new mechanics.

01

Predict-then-reveal worked examples

Oscillation Explorer's worked example draws a random curve, asks the student to read off A, T, and φ₀ themselves, then reveals the measurement. Most other sims' worked examples just show the solution immediately.

Good candidates: Energy in SHM, Equipotential Explorer, Gauss's Law.

02

Ghost / comparison overlays

Pendulum's "Ghost" toggle draws a dashed small-angle prediction alongside the real swing, so approximation error is something you watch happen. Reusable anywhere a sim has an exact model and a common approximation.

Candidates: Equipotential Explorer (point-charge vs. exact V), Standing Waves (ideal vs. damped), Universal Gravitation (circular vs. elliptical).

03

Discoverability of pan

E-Fields in Conductors is the only sim that prints "drag empty space to pan" on-screen. Every other sim with real pan support leaves it to be found by accident — a one-line addition, repeated everywhere pan already works.

04

Fill the Random-problem / Worked-example gaps

Circuit Lab and Radiation — A Wiggling Charge have no Random Problem generator, E-Fields in Conductors has none either, and Polarization — Malus's Law has no Worked Example.

05

Sound for the sound sims

Standing Waves and the new Beats/Doppler drafts are literally about audible phenomena. Doppler and Beats now have real Web Audio tones — Standing Waves and a future Resonance sim are the natural next candidates.

06

Conceptual checkpoint after Info

Nothing currently tests conceptual understanding (e.g., "which way does the field point here?"). A single multiple-choice checkpoint tied to the Info panel would close that loop cheaply — see the HW-quiz note below, since this is the same underlying feature.

05

Graphics pass

Started tonight. Drop & Toss's ball went from a flat fill to a radial-gradient glossy sphere (light source upper-left), matching the treatment already used for charges elsewhere — purely cosmetic, zero risk, shipped.

The coil-spring technique built for the SHM preview (layered drop shadow, perpendicular gradient, thin highlight stroke, all parametrized by direction so it works at any angle) is the reusable template for this pass — apply it anywhere a sim draws a mechanical object (blocks, strings, rods, pulleys, carts) with a flat fill today. Center of Mass and Relative Velocity's vector diagrams are the next clearest candidates in Mechanics.

06

Looking further out — upper-level & intro astro

Brainstorm only, per your ask — nothing here is scoped or started. Checked against the current 43 to avoid suggesting a duplicate; a few explicitly build on a sim or draft that already exists.

Upper-level physics

Special relativity — time dilation & length contraction

A light-clock animation in two frames side by side; γ slider shows the clock ticking slow / the ruler shrinking as v → c. Nothing in the current 43 touches relativity.

Relativistic Doppler & redshift

Extends the classical Doppler draft (already built, awaiting review) into the γ-corrected relativistic formula — natural bridge into the cosmological-redshift astro idea below.builds on: new-sims/doppler-effect.html

Photoelectric effect

Stopping voltage vs. frequency across several metals; slope gives h, x-intercept gives each metal's work function. The classic "light is quantized" evidence sim.

Bohr model & hydrogen spectrum

Electron jumping between allowed orbits, energy released as a photon at the matching visible/Balmer-series wavelength — makes discrete spectral lines concrete.

Quantum tunneling & particle in a box

Wavefunction hitting a finite barrier — transmission probability vs. barrier height/width; a box-well version showing quantized standing-wave states and |ψ|².

Radioactive decay & half-life

N(t) = N₀e^(−λt) as a population of nuclei decaying one at a time (discrete, not just the smooth curve) — ties into a binding-energy-curve companion for why some nuclei are unstable.

Driven & damped oscillator / RLC resonance

Amplitude vs. driving frequency with a resonance peak that sharpens as damping (or R, for the electrical analog) drops — one sim, two skins (mechanical spring-mass, series RLC), since they're the same equation.

Coupled oscillators & normal modes

Two masses on three springs — sliders for coupling strength show the symmetric/antisymmetric normal modes and, for unequal initial conditions, beating between them (nice pairing with the Beats draft).

Entropy & the second law

Free expansion of a small number of particles into a larger box, replayed many times — counting microstates makes "entropy increase" feel like counting, not magic.

Intro astronomy

Celestial sphere & coordinates

RA/Dec and alt/az on a rotating sphere with the observer's latitude as a slider — the prerequisite mental model for everything else in the unit.

Phases of the Moon

Sun–Earth–Moon geometry from a top-down orbit view and the corresponding Earth-sky view side by side — the single most common intro-astro misconception (it's geometry, not Earth's shadow) is easiest to fix by showing both views at once.

Eclipses — umbra & penumbra

Same three-body geometry as phases, but with the alignment tight enough to cast a shadow; slider for the Moon's orbital tilt shows why eclipses aren't monthly.

Seasons — axial tilt, not distance

Earth orbiting with its axis fixed in space; a light-intensity-per-area readout at a chosen latitude, to directly kill the "closer to the Sun in summer" misconception.

Retrograde motion

Same two-orbit geometry plotted from the Sun's frame and from Earth's sky at once — Mars looping backward stops being mysterious once you see both frames together.

Stellar parallax → distance ladder

Baseline (Earth's orbit) and parallax angle set distance; chain it into a "distance ladder" sim that hands off from parallax to standard candles to Hubble's law as distance grows past each method's range.

H-R diagram & blackbody color

Wien's law linking a star's temperature to its peak-emission color, plotted live against luminosity on an H-R diagram as you drag a star along the main sequence. Shares its blackbody-curve machinery with the upper-level blackbody idea — one physics engine, two skins.

Cosmological redshift & Hubble's law

Recession velocity from redshift, v = H₀d — directly reuses the Doppler draft's frequency-shift visualization, relabeled for light instead of sound.builds on: new-sims/doppler-effect.html

Exoplanet detection — transit & radial velocity

A planet crossing its star dims the light curve by (r_planet/r_star)²; the star's own wobble shows up as a periodic Doppler shift in its spectrum — two real detection methods, one system.

Telescope resolving power

Rayleigh criterion θ = 1.22λ/D — reuses the existing diffraction-pattern sim's single-slit/circular-aperture machinery, reframed as "can this telescope separate these two stars."builds on: diffraction-pattern/

Quiz-wise, the upper-level topics lend themselves to derivation-style questions (show the algebra step that gets you from A to B) rather than just numeric plug-in, which the current Worked-Example format doesn't really support yet — worth keeping in mind if/when the HW-quiz feature (next-steps #6 below) gets designed, since upper-level and intro-astro quizzes will probably need a different question type than the intro sims do.

7

Math-prerequisite practice activities not started — flagged only

A separate track from the physics sims: students need to actually practice the underlying math, not just have it explained. Seven topics called out — algebraic manipulation, geometry, trig, differentiation, integration, vector addition/subtraction, and dot & cross products. Explicitly not all screen-based — some of this is better as paper/whiteboard/physical activities than a browser exercise, so this needs its own design pass rather than reusing the sim template. Assessment shape: a diagnostic to check whether a student already knows the material; if not, route them into an adaptive quiz or practice sequence that builds toward a badge on completion. Needs scoping (screen vs. non-screen split per topic, what "adaptive" means here, how a badge is tracked/displayed) before any building starts — holding until you're ready to prioritize it.

Two separate capabilities to scope, both requiring you to be signed into Pearson's Mastering Physics in an interactive/browser session (not something doable headlessly with just an API key):

This is read-only against your own course data, so the main open question is just mechanics — what the export/print/copy options in Mastering Physics's UI actually expose, and whether a browser-automation pass (e.g. Claude with computer use, or a Playwright script driven by you interactively logging in) is the right tool versus doing it by hand.

2. Auto-create, current course. This is a write path against a live, currently-in-use gradebook — needs more care: confirming Mastering Physics actually supports a "visible from" / "invisible until" release-date field (not just a due date), and a dry-run or review step before anything goes live so a scripting mistake can't accidentally expose or misdate a real assignment.

For both: needs a working session to actually poke at the Mastering Physics UI together (screen-shared or with you driving the login) before committing to an approach — holding until you're ready to prioritize it.
