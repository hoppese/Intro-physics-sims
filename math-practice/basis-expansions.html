Expansions in a Basis ×

The idea

Approximate the same target function with an N-term sum from different families:

Taylor — powers 1, x, x², … about x = 0. Great locally; diverges outside its radius of convergence.
Fourier — sin(nπx), cos(nπx) (period 2). Best for smooth periodic data; Gibbs overshoot at jumps.
Chebyshev — Tn(x) = cos(n arccos x). Near-minimax: the max error shrinks fast and evenly. Nodes cluster at ±1.
Legendre — Pn(x). The truncation is the least-squares (L²) polynomial fit on [−1, 1].
Read the chart

The bottom panel is max error vs number of terms, on a log scale. A straight steep line means fast (often exponential) convergence; a line that flattens or turns up means trouble — a jump (Gibbs), a nearby singularity, or a diverging Taylor series.

The classic case

f(x) = 1/(1 + 25x²) — the Runge function. Its Taylor series only converges for |x| < 1/5, and an equal-spaced polynomial fit oscillates wildly near the ends. Chebyshev sails through.
