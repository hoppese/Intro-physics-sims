\noteshead{More Gauss's Law --- the Three Symmetries}{}

\goal{State Gauss's law and understand what it says, then use it to \emph{compute}
$\vec{E}$ --- not just check it --- for the distributions where symmetry makes that
possible: a point charge and a shell, an infinite line, an infinite sheet, and a
uniformly charged sphere.}

%============================================================
\nsec{What Gauss's law says}

\textbf{a) Flux.}
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=9pt,parsep=4pt,topsep=4pt]
\item The number of electric field lines that exit or enter a \bl[5.2cm]\ is the same,
no matter the \bl[4.4cm]\ of the surface. By ``number of field lines'' we mean \emph{flux}:
\eqline{$\Phi = $}
\item The surface's area vector is \bl[4.0cm]\ to the surface and points \bl[3.2cm], by
convention.
\item If lines are exiting, $\Phi$ is \bl[3.0cm]; if entering, $\Phi$ is \bl[3.0cm].
\item A line that just passes through counts $+1$ for leaving and $-1$ for entering, so
it contributes \bl[2.4cm]\ net.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{b) Relation to charge.}
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=9pt,parsep=4pt,topsep=4pt]
\item The number of field lines --- also called \bl[4.4cm] --- through a closed surface
is equal to \bl[6.0cm].
\item Does it matter how far from the charge the surface is? \bl[2.6cm]
\item The closed surface we integrate over is called a \bl[5.2cm].
\end{itemize}

sphere's radius, does $\Phi$ change? Does $E$ at the surface change? (Different questions!)}

\textbf{b)}
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=9pt,parsep=4pt,topsep=4pt]
\item Choose a Gaussian surface that respects the \bl[4.0cm]\ of the charge
distribution. If the distribution is spherically symmetric, make your surface
spherically symmetric too, \bl[6.4cm]\ (similarly for cylindrical and planar symmetry).
\item If you've done this, $\vec E$ will be everywhere either \bl[4.4cm]\ or
\bl[4.4cm]\ to your Gaussian surface, and $\Phi=E\oint dA=EA$ --- Gauss's law becomes
plain algebra: $EA=q_{\text{enc}}/\varepsilon_0$.
\end{itemize}
