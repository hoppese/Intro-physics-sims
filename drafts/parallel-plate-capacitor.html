Parallel-Plate Capacitor ×

What this shows

Two charged plates store energy in the field between them. Capacitance C depends only on geometry (area, separation) and what fills the gap — not on charge or voltage.

How to use

Compare the two modes: with the battery connected, V stays fixed and Q changes; disconnected, Q is trapped and V changes instead. Slide in the dielectric either way and watch which quantity moves.

Relevant formulas
capacitance — geometry and dielectric only
charge stored at voltage V
energy stored in the field
uniform field between the plates

Worked example — inserting a dielectric ×

A parallel-plate capacitor with A = 100 cm² and d = 2.0 mm is connected to a 9.0 V battery. Find C and Q, then slide in a κ = 5 dielectric (battery still connected) and find the new Q.

1 · Capacitance in vacuum
2 · Charge at 9.0 V
3 · Insert the dielectric (κ = 5), battery still connected — V stays 9.0 V
4 · New charge
5× the capacitance pulls 5× the charge onto the plates at the same voltage — the battery does work moving that extra charge.
If it had been disconnected first…
Q would stay at 3.99 nC (nowhere for charge to go) and V would instead drop to 1/5 of 9.0 V = 1.8 V. Same dielectric, opposite thing changes — that's the pairing to remember.
