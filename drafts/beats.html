What this shows

Two tones close in frequency drift in and out of phase. Where they agree the sum is loud; a moment later they cancel and it's quiet — a slow pulsing called beats, at a rate equal to the difference in frequency.

How to use

Set the two frequencies close together and hit play. Watch the envelope on the sum wave, and listen for the "wa-wa-wa" pulsing — count how many pulses per second and compare to f_beat below.

Relevant formulas

Worked example — tuning a guitar string ×

A guitar string is supposed to ring at 220 Hz. Played against a 220 Hz tuning fork, you hear 3 beats per second. Find the two possible string frequencies, then say which way to turn the tuning peg.

1 · The beat frequency only gives the difference
2 · Two possibilities
3 · Break the tie — tighten the string a little and listen
Tightening always raises pitch. If the beat rate rises, the string was the higher one (223 Hz) and you're moving further apart — loosen instead. If the beat rate falls, the string was 217 Hz and tightening is closing the gap — keep going until the beats disappear.
