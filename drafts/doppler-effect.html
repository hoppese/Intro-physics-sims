Doppler Effect ×

What this shows

A moving source (or observer) hears a shifted frequency: higher while approaching, lower while receding — the same wavefronts, just bunched up ahead and stretched out behind.

How to use

Hit Play and watch the wavefront rings. Switch between a moving source and a moving observer — same formula, different variable. Play the tone to hear the shift as the source (or observer) crosses the listener.

Relevant formulas
general Doppler shift — v is the speed of sound in the medium
moving source only: + receding, − approaching
moving observer only: + approaching, − receding

Worked example — an ambulance passing by ×

An ambulance siren emits 500 Hz. It approaches you at 30 m/s, then passes and recedes at the same speed. Speed of sound v = 343 m/s. Find what you hear on each side.

1 · Approaching — wavefronts bunch up ahead of the source
2 · Receding — wavefronts stretch out behind it
3 · The jump at the moment it passes
548 Hz drops straight to 460 Hz — an 88 Hz jump you hear as a sudden downward "whoop" right as it passes, even though the ambulance's own speed never changes.
