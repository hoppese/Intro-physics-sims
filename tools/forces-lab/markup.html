Forces Lab: build it, predict it, run it
What this shows
  • Each block is a particle with a size: every force on it acts at its centre, and it never tips or spins. It rests on a surface (floor, ramp, wall, ceiling) or on another block, hangs from ropes, is pulled by springs and applied forces, or flies free.
  • At every instant the lab solves Newton’s second law for all the blocks at once, with the rules of contact: a surface only pushes (the normal force), static friction holds up to μs n and no more, a sliding block feels kinetic friction μk n, and a rope only pulls, with one tension all along it. The answers are exact: they match the textbook formulas.
  • Ropes and pulleys are ideal (no mass, no friction, no stretch); springs obey Hooke’s law. A block landing on something stops dead (a perfectly inelastic bump). There is no air resistance.
  • Not modelled: rolling, tipping and spinning, massive pulleys and ropes, and friction that depends on speed.
How to use
  • + Add puts a block, a surface, a pulley or an anchor on the bench. For a rope, click a block or anchor, then each pulley it goes over, then its other end; a spring joins two things; an applied force pushes one block. Presets loads a classic problem.
  • Drag anything to move it. A block settles onto the surface or block it is dropped on (hold Shift to place it freely). Drag a surface’s gold handle to tilt and stretch it, an applied force’s to aim it.
  • Click a block to see every force on it, with its size and direction, the net force and the acceleration. The forces are worked out before you press Run, too.
  • ▶ Run (or Space) starts the clock; ↺ goes back to the start; ⏭ steps on; 1× switches to slow motion. Moving anything puts the clock back to zero.
  • Hide forces takes away every arrow and value, so students draw the free-body diagram first. 📏 Tape and ∠ Angle measure lengths and angles. View splits forces into components.
  • Keys: arrows nudge, R turns a surface, D copies, Delete removes, Ctrl/⌘ + Z undoes, Esc stops joining or deselects.
