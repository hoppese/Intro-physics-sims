Motion Lab: motion along a line
What this shows
  • Motion along a straight line, before forces come in: position x, velocity vx and acceleration ax, each a signed number (positive toward +x, to the right).
  • Match the graph: drive a cart in front of a motion sensor so its x–t or v–t graph follows a target, or watch a motion and sketch its graph.
  • Two cars, one road: each with its own start, velocity and acceleration. A braking car (aₓ opposite to vₓ) slows to a stop and stays there: nothing pushes it backward. Where the x–t graphs cross, the cars meet.
  • Program the motion: build the a–t graph (or the v–t graph) from pieces; the other graphs follow. Where velocity pieces meet, vₓ jumps at once: the a–t graph marks it with a spike, since no finite acceleration does that.
  • Motion diagram: a dot every 0.5 s, with velocity arrows, and the signs that say whether it speeds up or slows down. The models are ideal; cars are drawn larger than life on a long road.
How to use
  • ▶ Run (or Space) starts the clock; ↺ goes back to the start; ⏭ steps; 1× switches to slow motion.
  • Drag along the graphs to move the clock to any moment already run.
  • Driving: drag the cart (let go and it stops: nothing keeps it going), or set the throttle; hold → or ← to change the velocity steadily, ↓ to stop.
  • Sketching: press and drag across the marked graph to draw; ✓ Check shows the real graph and a score.
  • Slope & area draws the tangent to x–t (its slope is vx) and shades the area under v–t (Δx) and a–t (Δvx).
