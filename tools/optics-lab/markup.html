Optics Lab: build a ray diagram
What this shows
  • Light leaves a source in straight rays. Each thing a ray meets applies one rule: a mirror reflects it, a glass surface refracts it by Snell’s law, an ideal thin lens bends it toward or away from its axis.
  • A laser is one ray of one colour. A white source sends eleven colours along the same line. Glass has a slightly larger index for violet than for red, so a prism spreads white light into a spectrum.
  • A point source stands for one point of an object. Where its rays cross again is a real image (filled dot); where they only seem to come from is a virtual image (hollow dot, dashed lines). The side panel gives s, s′ and m for each lens or mirror. An object (an arrow, a tree or a dog) sends rays from its tip, and its whole image is drawn, so you can see whether it is upright or inverted, larger or smaller.
  • Lenses and mirrors start ideal: every ray obeys the thin-lens or mirror equation, however far from the axis. Tick Real to make them true spherical surfaces (a lens becomes a piece of glass refracting at both faces): rays far from the axis miss the focus (spherical aberration), and a strong real lens cannot be very tall. Tick Chromatic to let a lens’s glass disperse, so each colour has its own focal length (chromatic aberration). A mirror has none; prisms and blocks always disperse.
  • Not modelled: diffraction, interference, polarization, and absorption in the glass. The bench is a flat slice through the apparatus.
How to use
  • + Add Element puts a source, lens, mirror, piece of glass or screen on the bench. Presets loads a finished set-up to start from.
  • Drag an element to move it (it snaps to 1 cm; hold Shift for 0.1 cm). Drag its gold handle to turn it (5° steps; Shift for 1°). Drag a lens's or mirror's F (or a mirror's C) to change its focal length; pull F through the lens and it becomes diverging.
  • Click an element to type exact values: position, angle, focal length, index of refraction, wavelength.
  • Keys for the selected element: arrows nudge, R turns, D copies, Delete removes. Ctrl/⌘ + Z undoes, Esc deselects.
  • 📏 Tape (bottom of the bench) puts out a tape measure: drag its ends to pull it out (0.5 cm steps; Shift for 0.1 cm). ∠ Angle measures the angle between two lines: click two rays or flat surfaces (a mirror, a lens, a glass face, the tape). The angle is the one on the side you clicked; the other side is given too.
  • Drag the empty bench to pan; scroll or pinch to zoom; ⤡ brings everything back into view.
  • Real and Chromatic (beside New) switch every lens and mirror between ideal and real. View turns on extended rays, the angles at each surface (for a single ray), and the faint partial reflections.
  • Hide rays stops every ray at the first thing it meets, so you can predict before you look. Share link saves the whole bench in a URL.
