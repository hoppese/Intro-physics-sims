Energy Track: kinetic and potential energy
What this shows
  • A ball or a coaster car released on a track you can reshape. As it runs, kinetic energy K and gravitational potential energy Ug trade places; without friction their total stays exactly the same, whatever the shape.
  • The energy line is the height the cart would reach if all of K + Ug were Ug. Where the track rises to meet it, K = 0: the cart turns back. If the line is above every hill ahead, it gets over.
  • The normal force depends on the curve: less than mg over a crest, more in a dip. A ball rests on the track, so where the track would have to pull it in it flies off; a coaster car's wheels clamp round the rail, so the rail can pull.
  • Friction (kinetic, μk; taken as also holding a cart at rest when the slope is gentle enough) turns mechanical energy into thermal energy: K + Ug + Eth stays the same. A hard landing puts energy there too.
  • The model: a point cart at the track (drawn as a ball resting on it), no air resistance, g = 9.8 m/s².
How to use
  • Pick a track on the right, or reshape it: drag the white points; double-click the track to add a point, or a point to remove it.
  • Drag the ball (or car) along the track to choose where it starts, or type the start height. Give it a start speed if you like.
  • Drag the Ug = 0 tag to put the zero of potential energy anywhere: Ug and the total change, but the motion, K and every change in Ug do not.
  • ▶ Run (or Space) starts the clock; ↺ Reset goes back; ⏭ steps; 1× switches to slow motion. Drag along a graph to go back to any moment.
  • Hide energies takes away every energy value, the bars and the energy line, so students work them out first.
