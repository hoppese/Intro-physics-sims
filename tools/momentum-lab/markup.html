Momentum Lab: momentum is shared, never lost
What this shows
  • Three worlds: carts on an air track (one dimension), pucks on an air table (two), and bodies in space pulling on each other by gravity.
  • Every force here acts between two bodies, equally and oppositely. So whatever happens (a bounce, a stick, an explosion, a rocket firing, an orbit) the total momentum stays the same, and the centre of mass moves in a straight line at a steady speed.
  • Kinetic energy is a different story: kept in an elastic collision (e = 1), partly lost in an inelastic one, mostly lost when they stick, and gained from a spring or a rocket’s fuel.
  • The models are ideal: no friction on the track or table, bodies that never spin, and collisions as stiff springs that squash a little (bumpers about a centimetre, pucks a few millimetres) and push back. On big scales the small bodies are drawn larger than life.
How to use
  • Pick a world and a preset on the right. Click a body to type its mass, starting position and velocity ; + Add puts in another.
  • Before the start, drag a body to move it, and drag the tip of its arrow to set its velocity. Any change starts again from t = 0.
  • ▶ Run (or Space) starts the clock; ↺ goes back; 1× switches to slow motion, down to a twentieth, to watch a collision happen.
  • The graphs show momentum, force or energy against time. On the force graph the shaded areas are the impulses; Close-up zooms in on the last collision.
  • Hide values hides everything after the start (arrows, numbers, graphs), so students predict first.
